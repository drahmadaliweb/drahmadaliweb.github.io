Early life & education

From traditional Islamic learning to university scholarship.

Dr. Ahmad Ali was born in 1969 in West Kalauzan, Lohagara, Chattogram, Bangladesh. His education developed through both the madrasa tradition and university study. He completed Fazil and Kamil in Hadith before pursuing Arabic at the University of Chittagong.

He earned his B.A. (Honours) and M.A. in Arabic from the University of Chittagong, securing First Class, First position in both degrees. He later completed a Ph.D. in Arabic Literature from the same university in 2002. His doctoral research, The Contribution of Women to Arabic Literature (From the Pre-Islamic Era to 1258 A.D.), examined the literary contributions of women across major periods of classical Arabic literature. By bringing together material scattered across classical sources, the study helped present the role of female poets and prose writers more systematically within Arabic literary history. Later in 2007, he undertook postgraduate training at King Saud University in Riyadh in the teaching of Arabic to non-native speakers.

Academic career

More than three decades in teaching and higher education.

Dr. Ali began his teaching career at Chunati Hakimia Kamil Madrasah, where he served as a Muhaddith and Vice Principal. In 1996, he joined the University of Chittagong as a Lecturer in the Department of Arabic and Persian. He was promoted to Assistant Professor in 1999, Associate Professor in 2003 and Professor in 2008, and now serves in the Department of Islamic Studies.

He served as Chairman of the Department of Islamic Studies from 2015 to 2016 and previously as Senior House Tutor of A. F. Rahman Hall. His teaching has also extended beyond the University of Chittagong through part-time academic service at International Islamic University Chittagong and Darul Ulum Kamil Madrasah.

Scholarship & authorship

Research across Islamic law, thought and Arabic studies.

Dr. Ali's scholarship spans Islamic jurisprudence and comparative fiqh, Islamic law, Aqidah and theology, Qur'anic studies, Hadith and Seerah, Arabic language and literature, Tasawwuf and spiritual ethics, Islamic social and political thought, Islamic banking and Shariah-related issues, and contemporary intellectual questions. His longer projects include multi-volume works on Bid'ah and Usulul Iman, as well as an ongoing Bengali tafsir project.

Several of his works have reached academic audiences beyond his own institution through university reading lists, instructional materials and scholarly research. His books have been cited or used in different universities and academic settings in Bangladesh, particularly in fiqh, Islamic law, Arabic studies and related fields.

Editorial & institutional service

Academic and professional service beyond teaching.

Alongside teaching and authorship, Dr. Ali has contributed to editorial, professional and institutional work. He has served in editorial leadership of Islami Ain O Bichar, a quarterly research journal on Islamic law and related scholarship, and as a member of the Shariah Supervisory Committee of Social Islami Bank.

His scholarly and educational affiliations have also included the Bangladesh Islamic Centre, the Bangladesh Institute of Islamic Thought, the Universal League of Islamic Literature, and other academic and community institutions.

Public scholarly engagement

Seminars, conferences and public scholarship.

Dr. Ali has maintained a long-standing role as a public scholar and speaker. Over the years, he has presented research papers and delivered lectures at numerous national and international seminars, conferences, academic discussions, religious gatherings, book events, training programs and community educational forums. His engagements have taken place in Dhaka, Chattogram and many other parts of Bangladesh, as well as in international settings.

His talks have addressed subjects including comparative fiqh, Islamic law, Qur'an and Hadith, Seerah, Aqidah, spiritual and ethical development, zakat and waqf, Islamic banking, madrasa education, Muslim social thought and contemporary intellectual issues. He has participated as a paper presenter, discussant, session chair, reviewer and invited speaker.

Among his international academic engagements was participation in a Ministry of Hajj symposium in Makkah in 2013. In Bangladesh, his public scholarly activity has continued through seminars, university programs, Seerah gatherings, educational conferences and discussions connected with his books and research.

Continuing work

Teaching, writing and research in a changing intellectual landscape.

Dr. Ali continues to teach, write and engage with questions of Islamic scholarship and contemporary life. His ongoing work includes Qur'anic interpretation, Islamic law and legal thought, faith and theology, Islamic banking and Shariah compliance, and modern intellectual and social questions.

Across his academic, literary and public work, he has sought to connect the classical Islamic scholarly tradition with the educational, legal, ethical and intellectual concerns of contemporary society.
